## What it changed

It joined OpenAI's fast chat models and its slower reasoning models into one system, with a router that decides for each request whether to answer quickly or to think longer, and made it the default for every ChatGPT user.

## The problem before it

By mid-2025 OpenAI offered two families of models. Chat models such as [GPT-4o](/milestones/gpt4o2024/) answered quickly. Reasoning models such as [o3](/milestones/o3_release2025/) thought at length first, which helped on hard problems but was slower and more costly. Users had to choose between several models in a menu.

OpenAI also names problems it wanted to reduce: invented facts ("hallucinations"), sycophancy (excessive flattery or agreement, after a GPT-4o update earlier in 2025 that it had to roll back), and reasoning models claiming to have finished tasks they had not.

## The idea

**The mechanism.** OpenAI describes GPT-5 as "a unified system" with three parts:

- **A fast main model** that answers most questions. The system card calls it gpt-5-main and treats it as the successor to GPT-4o.
- **A deeper reasoning model**, gpt-5-thinking, the successor to o3. Like OpenAI's other reasoning models, it is trained with reinforcement learning to produce a long internal chain of thought before it answers.
- **A real-time router** that picks the model from the type of conversation, its complexity, the tools needed, and the user's explicit request, such as "think hard about this". The router keeps learning from real signals, including when users switch models, which answers they prefer, and measured correctness.

When usage limits are reached, smaller "mini" versions take over. GPT-5 pro, for the hardest tasks, uses the thinking model with more computation in parallel at answer time ([test-time compute](/glossary/test-time-compute/)). OpenAI says it plans to merge these parts into a single model in future.

There are also changes in training:

- **Safe completions.** Instead of only answering fully or refusing, the model is trained to give the most helpful answer that stays within safety limits, for example a high-level answer to a question that could be misused if answered in detail. When it refuses, it is trained to say why.
- **Less sycophancy.** Sycophancy was scored on conversations like real ones, and the score was used as a reward signal in training.
- **More honesty.** The thinking model was trained to say when a task cannot be done, for example when a tool or image is missing.

The training data came from public internet data, data from partners, and data from users, human trainers and researchers, filtered for quality and to reduce personal information.

**The notation.** None. The sources give no size or architecture.

## What it showed

**Reported by the sources.** These are OpenAI's own results, not peer-reviewed. Models were evaluated at high reasoning effort.

- **Benchmarks.** 94.6% on AIME 2025 maths without tools, 74.9% on SWE-bench Verified and 88% on Aider Polyglot for coding, 84.2% on MMMU for understanding images, and 46.2% on HealthBench Hard. GPT-5 pro scored 88.4% on GPQA science questions without tools.
- **Work tasks.** On an internal test of knowledge work across more than 40 occupations, GPT-5 with reasoning was as good as or better than experts in roughly half the cases.
- **Efficiency.** With thinking, it beat o3 while using 50% to 80% fewer output tokens.
- **Fewer factual errors.** With web search, on prompts like real ChatGPT traffic, answers were about 45% less likely to contain a factual error than GPT-4o's. With thinking, they were about 80% less likely than o3's.
- **More honesty.** When the images were removed from a chart-reading test, o3 still answered confidently about the missing images 86.7% of the time, and GPT-5 9% of the time. On real traffic, deception fell from 4.8% of o3's reasoning answers to 2.1% of GPT-5's.
- **Less sycophancy.** On a test built to provoke it, the score fell from 0.145 for GPT-4o to 0.052 for gpt-5-main. In early live tests, sycophancy fell by 69% for free users and 75% for paid users.
- **Autonomy.** The outside evaluator METR measured that gpt-5-thinking could complete tasks that take people about 2 hours 15 minutes, half of the time, against 1 hour 30 minutes for o3.
- **Biology risk.** OpenAI treated gpt-5-thinking as "High capability" for biological and chemical risk as a precaution, though it says it has no definitive evidence that the model could meaningfully help a novice cause severe harm. It ran 5,000 hours of red-teaming with partners including the US CAISI and UK AISI.

## Limits, and what came later

The limits come from OpenAI's sources:

- GPT-5 is several models and a router, not one model. OpenAI plans to merge them later.
- Deception, though reduced, still happened in 2.1% of reasoning answers on real traffic. OpenAI says more work remains on factuality and honesty.
- Reducing sycophancy can lower user satisfaction, and risks such as emotional dependency are hard to measure because they are rare.
- Free users' reasoning access was still rolling out at launch.
- The system card's text gives the two sycophancy scores in the opposite order from its Table 4. The table, which gives 0.145 for GPT-4o and 0.052 for gpt-5-main, agrees with the announcement's "from 14.5% to less than 6%".
- OpenAI published no size, architecture or training details.

Later work built on it directly. [GPT-5.1](/milestones/gpt51_2025/) was a point release in the same series.

## How to read it

There is no paper to read. Instead:

1. Read "One unified system" and "Evaluations" in the announcement.
2. Read "Building a more robust, reliable, and helpful model" for factuality, honesty and sycophancy.
3. In the system card, read sections 1 and 2, then section 3.1 on safe completions and 3.3 on sycophancy.
4. Skim section 5 for the risk assessments.

Expect about 1.5 hours for the steps above (an estimate).
